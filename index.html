Who we are

A bridge between goodwill and surgery.

Half 4 Life is a charity project established in 2026 to bridge the systemic healthcare deficit in Western Uganda. From Mbarara, we serve poor patients from marginalised villages across Isingiro, Rwampara, Lyantonde, Kiruhura, Ibanda, Sheema, Ntungamo, Buhweju, and Kazo.

We build formal partnerships between volunteer surgical teams, accredited regional hospitals, and philanthropic partners. Together, we create a dignified, high-quality, and cost-effective pathway to general and reconstructive surgery.

Our work is simple to describe: no patient should lose their livelihood, their dignity, or their life because they cannot pay the upfront costs of an operation.

  • Volunteer surgeons waive their professional fees.
  • Lanz Clinic and partner hospitals provide subsidised theatre time and care.
  • Donors cover the non-surgical costs that patients cannot.

How it works

Six steps from village to recovery

Every patient follows a documented clinical and financial pathway, from first screening to their 30-day review.

01

Community identification & screening

Village health teams and regional clinics refer patients who cannot afford surgical care.

02

Clinical assessment & workup

Volunteer surgeons review each patient and arrange laboratory tests and imaging.

03

Financial clearance & enrolment

A social worker verifies poverty status and authorises the H4L subsidy.

04

Surgical intervention

The elective procedure is performed in a partner theatre under WHO safety protocols.

05

Inpatient recovery & discharge

Nursing care, pain management, and wound monitoring continue until safe discharge.

06

Post-discharge follow-up

Clinical reviews at 7, 14, and 30 days confirm recovery and check for infection.

Why people trust H4L

A model built for accountability and care.

We keep the process clear, clinical, and traceable so donors, clinicians, and families know exactly what support is being provided.

01 · Transparent costs

Every dollar is explained.

Our unit-cost model shows exactly what a donation covers, from theatre and anaesthesia to medicines, diagnostics, and the 30-day follow-up review.

02 · Clinical oversight

Care decisions remain clinical.

Volunteer surgeons, hospital teams, and local social workers assess each patient under the same clinical standards, with financial management kept separate.

03 · Community follow-up

The work continues after surgery.

Every patient is reviewed at 7, 14, and 30 days, helping protect recovery, reduce complications, and confirm that funds are being used well.

Featured procedure

Inguinal hernia repair

A symptomatic hernia can become a strangulated hernia — a life-threatening emergency. Timely repair prevents that crisis and allows adults to return to heavy manual work and provide for their families.

H4L covers the mesh, sutures, theatre fees, anaesthesia, diagnostics, medicines, and follow-up. The patient pays nothing.
