Half 4 Life is committed to safe, dignified, and respectful care. This statement explains the standards we expect from everyone who works with us and how to raise a concern.

Immediate danger? Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest hospital. Do not wait for a website or email response if someone is at immediate risk.

Our commitment

We have zero tolerance for abuse, exploitation, harassment, discrimination, or neglect. This applies to patients, their families, staff, volunteers, partners, and anyone who represents Half 4 Life. We aim to create an environment where people are treated with dignity and where concerns can be raised without fear.

Who this applies to

This statement applies to H4L team members, clinicians, volunteers, partner organisations, photographers, contractors, and visitors acting for or with H4L. Partners are expected to share our commitment to safeguarding and to follow their own professional and legal obligations.

Dignity, consent, and patient stories

We publish identifiable patient stories and photographs only with appropriate informed consent. Consent is specific: it covers the image or story, the context in which it will be used, and the platforms on which it may appear. Consent can be withdrawn, and we will remove or amend content where appropriate.

We do not publish images of sensitive conditions, identifiable children, or graphic surgical close-ups without a clear safeguarding and consent basis. Where a story is a composite, we label it as illustrative and change names and clinical details.

Children and vulnerable adults

Clinical care for children and vulnerable adults must follow Lanz Clinic’s professional policies and applicable Ugandan law. We do not publish identifiable images of children without documented consent from a parent or guardian. Anyone working with children or vulnerable adults is expected to act protectively and to report concerns promptly.

What happens next

We will take every concern seriously. The response may include immediate steps to protect the person at risk, review of records, discussion with clinical leadership, and referral to the appropriate authorities where required. Information will be shared only with people who need it. We may not be able to share every detail of an investigation with the person who reported it, but we will keep them informed where we can.

Review
