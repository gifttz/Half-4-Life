Tumour · Patient story

Clarity, closure, and a scar she can live with

18 February 2026 3 min read Half 4 Life team

Clinical team photo; not the patient described in this story.

She found the lump while bathing. It was small, movable, and painless. The internet offered a long list of possibilities. The nearest hospital offered an appointment she could not afford to keep.

A lump, and a lot of fear

Benign breast lesions are common. Most are not cancer. But “most” is not a diagnosis, and a young woman with a new lump should not have to choose between uncertainty and financial strain.

She had noticed the lump several months earlier. It was not growing quickly, but it was tender before her period and always present. She avoided seeking care because she assumed the tests would cost more than she could manage.

The value of histopathology

Clinical assessment can narrow the possibilities, but tissue examination provides clarity. H4L supported her excision and the histopathology that followed.

The procedure removed the lump and sent the tissue for laboratory analysis. The result was benign. That single word gave her something she had not had for months: certainty.

A small scar

The operation was performed under local or regional anaesthesia, depending on the clinical assessment. The incision was planned to follow the natural lines of the breast, keeping the scar as inconspicuous as possible.

She was discharged with a simple dressing plan and reviewed in the clinic. The wound healed well. The lump was gone, and so was the fear that had followed her into every mirror.

What it costs to remove uncertainty

The all-inclusive unit cost for a procedure like hers is $175. That covers the theatre, anaesthesia, consumables, diagnostics, medicines, and follow-up. The surgeon’s fee is donated.

For a woman who spent months worrying in silence, the value is not measured only in shillings. It is measured in the ability to plan a future again.
